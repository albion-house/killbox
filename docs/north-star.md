# KILLBOX NORTH STAR

## Rift Vision

Killbox is a strange, readable, chaotic co-op survival adventure about moving through dangerous places, protecting a living Rift, and creating ridiculous stories with another player.

The long-term dream is not just to make a tower defense game.

The dream is:

**Two players exploring a hostile world together, improvising defenses, rescuing each other, and barely surviving weird disasters.**

Killbox should feel like the game Joel wanted as a kid:

- Zelda, but you could bring a friend.
- Ikari Warriors, but with towers, monsters, and a haunted base you have to protect.
- A dungeon crawl where each room becomes a temporary battlefield.
- A survival comedy where bad plans become good stories.

---

## Two Different Experiences

Arena and Expedition are intentionally different.

Arena is:

- Fast
- Chaotic
- Escalating
- Power-fantasy focused

Expedition is:

- Exploration
- Discovery
- Resource management
- Risk and reward
- Adventure

Expedition should not simply become Arena with different maps.

The player should feel the difference immediately.

---

## The Player Fantasy

The player should feel like:

- “We found something dangerous.”
- “We need to hold this room.”
- “Put the Rift over there.”
- “Build walls before they come through that hallway.”
- “I’ll kite them, you repair.”
- “Don’t move the Rift yet.”
- “That was stupid, but it worked.”

The best Killbox moments should sound like stories players tell each other afterward.

---

## Long-Term Multiplayer North Star

Killbox should eventually support cooperative play.

Multiplayer should not feel like an extra mode bolted on later.

Design choices should avoid trapping the project in a solo-only corner.

The ideal future co-op loop:

1. Players enter a dangerous room or zone.
2. They place or protect the Rift.
3. The Rift creates the buildable safe or defense area.
4. Enemies attack from readable directions.
5. Players shoot, build, repair, kite, revive, and improvise.
6. The room is cleared.
7. The Rift is moved forward.
8. The team chooses where to make the next stand.

---

## Expedition Rhythm

Expedition should alternate between:

- Exploration
- Combat waves
- Dungeons
- Relays

That alternation is the point.

The player is not only clearing fights.
The player is advancing through a hostile world and deciding when to push, when to fortify, and when to risk more ground.

---

## The Rift

The Rift is the heart of Killbox.

It should eventually feel like:

- A mobile base
- A shared objective
- A build anchor
- A thing players argue about
- A thing players risk themselves to save
- The reason rooms matter

The Rift should not just be a marker.

The Rift should create decisions.

Good Rift gameplay:

- Players want to hide it.
- Players want to defend it.
- Players want to build around it.
- Players feel nervous when it is exposed.
- Players feel relief when it stabilizes.
- Players feel ownership over where it goes next.

---

## Expedition Mode Direction

Expedition should become the mode that best supports the long-term dream.

It should feel like Ikari Warriors plus Gauntlet plus Zelda-style exploration.

The overworld should not be downtime between dungeons.

The overworld is part of the adventure.

Expedition should emphasize:

- Exploration
- Curiosity
- Treasure
- Weapon upgrades
- Unexpected discoveries
- Multiplayer adventure

The player pushes through hostile territory, then temporarily turns part of it into civilization.

Expedition risk:

Do not become a sequence of isolated combat rooms.

Do not flatten Expedition into Arena on a bigger map.

The player should feel like they are advancing through territory.

Forward momentum is critical.

---

## Multiplayer-Friendly Design Rules

When adding systems, prefer mechanics that naturally support two players.

Good future-proof systems:

- Shared objectives
- Revives
- Defensible positions
- Chokepoints
- Rift placement
- Room clearing
- Enemy pressure from multiple angles
- Roles that emerge naturally
- Simple communication moments
- Visible danger
- Visible cooldowns
- Player rescue moments

Avoid systems that make future co-op harder:

- Heavy pause-based decisions
- Complicated inventories
- Hidden mechanics
- Solo-only scripted sequences
- Precision micromanagement
- Excessive UI
- Overly complex crafting
- Mechanics only one player can understand
- Long downtime between fights

---

## Design Pillars

### 1. Readable Chaos

The screen can be wild, but players should still understand what is happening.

### 2. Shared Survival

Even before multiplayer exists, systems should imagine a second player eventually standing nearby.

### 3. The Rift Matters

The Rift should drive movement, building, defense, and progression.

### 4. Rooms Create Stories

Rooms should not just be spaces. They should be situations.

### 5. Strong Choices Over Many Choices

A few meaningful decisions are better than many tiny upgrades.

---

## The Current Expedition Hypothesis

Expedition is evolving toward a loop where exploration and pressure alternate.

The current pressure-heavy dungeon style is valuable, but it should not be the only shape Expedition can take.

The best direction is a hybrid of:

- Exploration between fights
- Tactical pressure when rooms lock
- Treasure and upgrade discovery
- Relays as progression landmarks
- Flexible dungeon archetypes

The world tone should keep curiosity ahead of explanation. A player should notice the strange object first and understand it later.

If this is working, the player should feel curious before they feel optimal.

---

## What To Protect

When making changes, protect these qualities:

- Fast decision making
- Weirdness
- Readability
- Player stories
- Humor
- Emergent tactics
- Tower plus shooting hybrid identity
- Future co-op possibility
- Exploration breathing room

---

## Development Guidance

Before implementing a new feature, ask:

1. Does this create a memorable player story?
2. Does this make the Rift more important?
3. Does this support future co-op?
4. Does this improve readable chaos?
5. Does this add meaningful decisions?
6. Is there a simpler version we can test first?
7. Could this create UI clutter?
8. Could this trap the game in a solo-only design?

Prefer small prototypes that prove fun quickly.

Do not build large systems until the gameplay need is proven.

---

## One-Sentence North Star

**Killbox is a weird co-op survival adventure where players explore hostile territory, defend a living Rift, and create stories by surviving chaos together.**
