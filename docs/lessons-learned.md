# Lessons Learned

## Playtesting Findings

These are the current lessons from playtests and implementation reviews.

- Arena and Expedition are strongest when they feel different.
- Players remember identities more than mechanics.
- Players choose identities first.
- Players use stats to confirm the choice.
- Players use mechanics to master the choice.
- Weapon fantasy is more important than control descriptions.
- Information should be visible where decisions are made.
- Mystery creates curiosity.
- Exploration needs breathing room between moments of pressure.
- The player experience is the final authority, even when code reviews and tests pass.

---

## Design Takeaways

The most useful improvements so far have been the ones that make a mode feel like itself.

That means:

- Clear Arena power fantasy
- Clear Expedition adventure fantasy
- Strong room identity
- Visible decision points
- Fewer explanatory walls of text

If a system requires the player to open a secondary panel just to understand the main choice, the information is probably in the wrong place.

---

## Documentation Takeaway

Docs should support creative direction, not bury it.

They should preserve the reasons behind design choices, especially when those choices are about feel, identity, or pacing rather than raw mechanics.
